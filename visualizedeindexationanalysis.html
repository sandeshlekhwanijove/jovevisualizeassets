Readability Scores

Three overlapping readability metrics each penalise long sentences and complex vocabulary. This content is academic — all scores skew hard. The question is whether variance within that range predicts indexation.

Flesch Reading Ease — what it is
A 0–100 score penalising long sentences and polysyllabic words. Higher = easier to read. Academic writing typically scores 0–30.
206.835 − (1.015 × avg words/sent) − (84.6 × avg syllables/word)
Target: 20–50
Gunning Fog Index — what it is
Estimates reading difficulty from sentence length and percentage of complex words (3+ syllables). Score 12 = high school; 17+ = graduate. Penalises polysyllabic words harder than Flesch.
0.4 × (avg words/sentence + % words with 3+ syllables)
Target: 12–16
FK Grade Level — what it is
Maps difficulty to a US school grade number. Grade 12 = high school graduate. Grade 16 = college senior. Grade 18+ = postgraduate. Same inputs as Flesch, different weighting.
(0.39 × avg words/sent) + (11.8 × avg syllables/word) − 15.59
Target: Grade 13–15

Lexical Diversity

Type-Token Ratio: proportion of unique words to total words. A score of 0.50 means half the words are repeated. This is the strongest and most consistent predictor in the dataset.

Type-Token Ratio (TTR) — what it is
The ratio of unique words (types) to total words (tokens). A TTR of 0.50 means half of all words in the text are repeated. A score of 0.70 means 70% of words appear only once. Higher TTR = richer, more varied vocabulary. The single strongest indexation predictor in the entire dataset — a monotonic relationship where every step up raises the index rate. Primary cause of low TTR: boilerplate methodology text repeated across hundreds of articles.
Formula
unique words ÷ total words
e.g. 420 ÷ 700 = 0.60

Language & Link Signals

Meta Title — what it is
The clickable blue headline in Google results. Lives in the HTML <title> tag. First signal Google reads. Primary keyword must appear in the first 40 characters. Google truncates beyond ~60 chars.
Target: 50–60 characters → 66.5% index rate
Meta Description — what it is
The grey text below the title in search results. Does not directly affect ranking but directly controls click-through rate (CTR). Must lead with the keyword or finding — not "This study…" — to maximise relevance signals.
Target: 150–160 characters
E-E-A-T + Semantic Density
E-E-A-T (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness) is Google's quality framework. Semantic density = richness of relevant terminology on a page. Both signal genuine subject coverage, not keyword stuffing.

Source — PubMed Abstract (input to both prompts)

"Variation in immune homeostasis, the state in which the immune system is maintained in the absence of stimulation, is highly variable across populations. This variation is attributed to both genetic and environmental factors. However, the identity and function of specific regulators have been difficult to identify in humans. We evaluated homeostatic antibody levels in the serum of the Collaborative Cross (CC) mouse genetic reference population. We found heritable variation in all antibody isotypes and subtypes measured. We identified 4 quantitative trait loci (QTL) associated with 3 IgG subtypes: IgG1, IgG2b, and IgG2c. While 3 of these QTL map to genome regions of known immunological significance (major histocompatibility and immunoglobulin heavy chain locus), Qih1 (associated with variation in IgG1) mapped to a novel locus on Chromosome 18. We further associated this locus with B cell proportions in the spleen and identify Methyl-CpG binding domain protein 1 under this locus as a novel regulator of homeostatic IgG1 levels in the serum and marginal zone B cells (MZB) in the spleen, consistent with a role in MZB differentiation to antibody secreting cells."

Example 1 — Background Section · TTR, Boilerplate & Prose Quality
Original prompt — actual output

• Immune homeostasis, crucial for a balanced immune system, exhibits significant population-level variation.
• Genetic and environmental factors contribute to this variation, but specific regulators remain largely unidentified in humans.
• Understanding these regulators is key to addressing immune dysregulation.

Bullet list — not prose TTR ~0.45 Flesch ~6 Fog ~24 Predicted index rate: ~28–34%
Suggested prompt — actual output

Identifying the genetic regulators of immune homeostasis has long outpaced the tools available in human cohort studies. Genetic and environmental variables co-vary so extensively in natural populations that isolating a single molecular driver remains technically prohibitive. Controlled mammalian models with high hereditary diversity offer a route past this bottleneck — enabling researchers to decouple inherited immune set points from environmental confounders and map specific loci to baseline antibody concentrations.

Flowing prose ✓ TTR ~0.64 Flesch ~24 Fog ~17 Predicted index rate: ~50–56%
What changed and why it matters
Original: bullets collapse TTR to 0.45
3 bullet fragments share "variation," "genetic," "factors," "regulators" across just ~60 words. TTR hits ~0.45 — the worst band in the dataset at 28% index rate. Paragraph density per 100 words also spikes (negative predictor: −0.296). Fog score of ~24 puts it in the worst readability band at 37.5% indexed.
Suggested: gap-first prose with fresh vocabulary
"co-vary," "bottleneck," "decouple," "confounders," "prohibitive" are all unique words not in the source abstract and not reused later in the article. Opens with the specific problem (human cohort tool limitations) rather than a generic field overview. TTR moves to ~0.64 → the 53%+ index rate band. Flesch moves from 6 to 24 → the 48% indexed band.
Example 2 — Main Methods Section · Active Voice, Sentence Length & Vocabulary Isolation
Original prompt — actual output

• Utilized the Collaborative Cross (CC) mouse genetic reference population to assess serum antibody levels.
• Employed quantitative trait loci (QTL) mapping to identify genetic regions associated with antibody variation.
• Analyzed B cell proportions in spleen and identified candidate genes under significant QTL.

Bullet list Fog verbs: utilized (4 syl), analyzed (4 syl) FK Grade ~19
Suggested prompt — actual output

The Collaborative Cross (CC) population supplied the genetic breadth needed to resolve complex immunological traits at chromosomal scale. Serum IgG levels across all strains were measured with standardised assays, yielding heritability estimates for each isotype. Linkage mapping traced four genomic sites to antibody variance, with Chromosome 18 singled out for targeted follow-up. Splenic B cell subsets were profiled by flow cytometry to connect each locus with a concrete cellular phenotype.

Flowing prose ✓ Avg 21w/sent No Background verb repeats FK Grade ~14
What changed and why it matters
Original: polysyllabic bullet verbs inflate Fog
"Utilized" (4 syl), "Employed" (2 syl), "Analyzed" (4 syl) are all complex words penalised by Gunning Fog. The bullet format also fragments prose into isolated token islands, preventing cross-sentence vocabulary variety. FK Grade ~19 sits in the 42.5% indexed band.
Suggested: short active verbs, isolated vocabulary
"supplied," "resolve," "traced," "singled out," "profiled" — all 1–2 syllable verbs not used in the Background section. Average sentence length drops to 21 words. FK Grade moves from ~19 to ~14, directly entering the 44.5% indexed band. Cross-section vocabulary isolation sustains whole-page TTR.

Original prompt — actual output

No meta title generated. No FAQs generated. No primary/secondary keyword identification. Meta description exists in the prompt spec but is not structured as a separate optimised output.

What changed and why it matters
Original: structurally missing key elements
Without a meta title, Google generates its own from the H1 — typically not keyword-optimised. Without FAQs, the page misses all long-tail query coverage and is ineligible for FAQ schema rich results in the SERP. The TL;DR "Genetic factors drive variation…" is a common pattern across thousands of science summaries — it will not differentiate this page in search.
Suggested: every SERP element optimised
The 52-char title sits in the 66.5% indexed band vs 43% for longer titles. The description opens with "MBD1 drives…" — an active verb with the discovery in position 1, maximising click-through rate. Each of the 6 FAQs adds a unique vocabulary cluster to the page's whole-word token count, compounding the TTR gains from the prose sections and making the page matchable to a wider set of distinct search queries.
